## Site 4 — the un-approval cascade

You are right that it is not an invariant. Invariants are floors and walls — things the agent must never do or must always do. This is state semantics: what the word "approved" means when the user goes back. I mis-shelved it.

Where I land after looking harder: the six gates do not actually each need "and Y not approved" *because* of the cascade — they need it to de-hold, which is a separate requirement. The cascade is what makes them re-hold. Different jobs, and only the second is homeless.

Four ways to home it.

**Option A — a Terms entry.** Define the word the conditions already lean on.

```markdown
- **Approval** — the user's sign-off on a phase artifact. Revising an approved artifact un-approves it and every artifact produced after it in the build, so those phases' conditions hold again.
```

Cheapest, one home, and a cold reader hits it exactly where they hit the unfamiliar word. The spec-check's `Terms earn their place` is satisfied hard — six start conditions lean on it. Weakness: a Term is passive. It tells the agent what state to read, never prompts it to walk the cascade, so a revision at `+Review` may quietly un-approve one artifact and leave the map still marked good.

**Option B — a dormant step.** Give the cascade its own step.

```markdown
## +Un-approve Downstream Work

Walk back the approvals a revision invalidates, and tell the user what re-opened.

**Dormant step** — Skippable, activates only when its state arises.

#### Start this step when these are true:

The user is revising an artifact that was already approved.

#### Step finished when these are true:

The revised artifact and every artifact produced after it in the build are un-approved, and the user has been told which phases re-opened.
```

Dormant fits the catalogue exactly — most builds never revise backwards, and a run that never enters it behaves correctly. It makes the cascade *happen* rather than merely be readable, and it names no other step (the phase order does the work). Weakness: a step whose whole engagement is a state edit is thin, and `Edges at real boundaries` could read it as over-splitting.

**Option C — routing step.** Your other suggestion. I do not think it holds. The catalogue says a routing step's *work is the choice*, and here there is no choice — the revised artifact determines what re-opens. Nothing is decided.

**Option D — A plus B.** Term carries the meaning, step carries the walk. No duplication if the Term states the semantics and the step states the actions.

## What I would pick

B, alone. It closes the gap actively, and its finished condition makes the un-approval observable — which is what the gate conditions need to read. A is the safer minimum but leaves the cascade as something the agent must infer at exactly the moment it is least likely to.

If the thin engagement bothers you, D — but the Term then earns its place on cold-readability, not on carrying the fact.

One thing worth naming: whatever we choose here becomes the pattern for `versioning`, which has the same gate shape and the same silent dependency in wave 5.

Separately, the abandoned-build half of site 4 is untouched by this choice. That repair stands as recommended — `+Handle a Problem`'s finished conditions stating the ended build, plus its `**Error step**` declaration.

De-hold on the artifact the step leaves behind, never on the step's own completion. A step that leaves no observable trace either needs one, or should not be a separate step.

## The scoping phrases — dropping all five

The jam I was defending against was real in the *old* model: de-hold clauses were process-negations ("has not passed the scenario-walk") that no amount of reading could invalidate, because nothing observable corresponds to them. Once de-hold goes and every finish is an artifact fact, an agent looking at a revised reference set and a map approved before it can tell the map is stale. That is ordinary reading, not machinery.

It is also the convention this framework just adopted. Commit 6c92e14 chose the directive specifically "to lean on the model's own reading of the word rather than rules suppressing the wrong behaviour", and named bloat toward code as the danger. Five clauses buying an edge case a competent agent already handles is exactly that bloat. Diffs 2, 4, 5 and 6 lose their scoping.

Diff 7 is redundant for a different reason: `+Deliver`'s finish already carries "re-walk anything an edit touched", so the re-walk after a user edit is owned there. `+Review` needs no scoping to reach it.
